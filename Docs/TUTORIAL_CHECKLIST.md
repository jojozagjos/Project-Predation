# Tutorial checklist

Everything a new player has to learn, for brainstorming the tutorial. Mechanics only: the story around it is the
user's to write. Grouped roughly in the order a player meets them. **Must** is needed to finish a first run;
**should** is needed to do well; **later** can be learned by playing.

## On your feet (must)

- Looking and moving; sprint and the stamina bar (it runs out, it comes back).
- Walk (quiet) vs sprint (loud): the creature hears footsteps.
- Crouch and prone: getting under things, being harder to see.
- Lean left and right: looking round a corner without stepping out.
- Jump.

## Seeing (must)

- The torch: on and off, the battery bar, dimming and stuttering near the end, swapping a battery cell in.
- The torch makes you visible: light is a trade-off, not free.
- Flares: strike, hold, throw; they light a room *and* draw attention when they land.

## Carrying (must)

- Interact (pick up, open doors, use consoles).
- The hotbar: slots 1-6, scrolling, the name shown when you land on an item.
- The inventory screen; dropping an item.
- Fire uses whatever is in your hand (med kit, battery, keycard, flare).
- The keycard has to be held, not just carried, to unlock a door.

## Fighting (should)

- Fire, aim down sights, reload; ammo crates.
- Friendly fire (if the host has it on).
- The creature is tough: fighting is a last resort, not the plan.

## The ship (must)

- The loadout locker in the gear room: choosing what to take down.
- The shuttle in the bay: everybody aboard, then the launch.
- The ops room's screens (where the ship is going, where it is, whether the shuttle is ready).

## The site (must)

- Finding the building; the map, when one is given, and what to do without one.
- The terminal: starting the download, staying near it while it runs, the noise it makes.
- Power out: finding the breaker panel, and that resetting it is loud.
- The data drive: picking it up, carrying it to the shuttle, that it drops if you die.
- Getting back to the shuttle and launching home.

## The creature (should)

- It hunts by sound and light: noise from steps, doors, gunfire, flares, the terminal, the breaker.
- Hiding spots: getting in, staying still, getting out.
- It copies voices: a teammate's voice is not proof it is a teammate.
- The nest: what it is, that it can be hurt and killed, that a nest dies slowly.
- Being grabbed and cocooned at the nest; freeing a teammate from a cocoon.

## Together (should)

- Voice chat: push to talk, and that it carries by distance.
- Healing a teammate with a med kit (look at them, in reach).
- Splitting up vs staying together.

## Dying (later)

- On a site, death is for good: you come back as a drone.
- The drone: driving it, what it can still do for the team.
- Dying aboard the ship: back on your feet in a few seconds.

## The campaign and the ship (must, now built)

- The navigation table in the ops room: opening the system map, turning and zooming it, picking out a planet or moon.
- What is known and what is not: a world's details fill in as the ship scans it or the crew goes down.
- Setting a course; walking the ship while it flies; changing the course or calling it off part way.
- Pointing things out to the others on the map (they see what you point at).
- Arriving in orbit: the scan finds places to land; choosing one; the shuttle in the bay.
- Saving: the host's job (the pause menu), and that the game autosaves on getting back aboard.

## Later (once built)

From the open-exploration design: landing regions of other kinds of world, salvage and resources, credits and
components, the shipyard and upgrading the ship, the ship's log, CIRRA's contracts.

## Open questions for the tutorial

- Where it happens (on the ship, a training site, a real first site) and who or what is teaching.
- Whether it is solo, co-op, or both.
- Whether the creature appears in it, and how scary the first meeting should be.
- How much is taught up front vs by intercom lines or on-screen hints during the first real run.
