# Gameplay — what takes away from it, and what would make it better (2026-11-24)

You asked: right now it feels like *follow the objective tracker (which only shows one thing), find the thing, do the task*, and
sometimes it piles tasks on you. Below is what I think causes that, what the games this one sits beside do about it, and what I propose.
Each numbered item ends with a question for you (**Q**) and my recommendation. Nothing here is built until you answer.

What the genre's best-loved games get right, from reviews and design write-ups: a short, tense loop with a clear goal and stakes; a
vulnerable crew; failure as part of the loop, not the end of it; places that differ run to run; and co-op that makes people talk to
each other. Their commonest criticism is repetition once the loop is learnt. On navigation, design research is mixed: a marker on the
target is the easy fallback and the least immersive; marking the *way* (landmarks, signs, a path) or giving only a bearing reads as more
"found", and no one display is best for everything.

---

## A. What takes away from it now

### 1. The tracker does the finding for you
The objective tracker points at the next thing, with its distance. Everything else — the map, the signs, the rooms' purposes, notes
— is then optional: the fastest play is to walk where the needle says. And it shows one thing at a time, so you never plan.

**Proposal:** the tracker stops pointing at things nobody has found. It knows the *site* (the pad, the buildings, their names from the
briefing's map), and once anyone has *seen* a thing (a breaker board, a fuse box, the terminal), it remembers it and can point back to
it. Finding is done with what is in the world: the site map (rooms labelled by purpose), building signs, room signs on doors, and clues
(a note says "fuel in the stores, Block C"). The objectives list shows **all the steps known so far** at once, ticked as they go,
not one line.

**Q1.** Tracker only for what has been found, and all known steps listed? *(recommended)* — or keep pointing at everything but list
all steps — or drop the tracker altogether?

### 2. Too many tasks stacked on one mission
A data mission can roll power out + feed cut + supply fault + a locked terminal + a damaged rack, all at once, each its own chain of
steps — and on the first contracts too. That is where "it overwhelms you" comes from: the complications are independent dice.

**Proposal:** a **complication budget**. Each mission gets a number of complications set by how hard the area rates (Difficulty: how
far out, the threat) — 0–1 on the set contracts near home, 1–2 normally, 3 far out — and draws that many from those that fit the place.
The briefing says roughly what to expect ("power reported out"), so it is a plan, not a surprise list.

**Q2.** Budget by difficulty as above? *(recommended)* — or a fixed one or two per mission?

### 3. The site is a list of chores, and staying costs nothing
Once you know the steps, there is no reason to hurry and no reason to linger. The creatures are mostly something that happens to you
while doing chores.

**Proposal:** **pressure that rises the longer you stay and the louder you are** — what lives there grows more active (more of them
about, bolder) as time on site and noise add up; dusk comes on at sites with a day cycle; weather turns. Leaving early is always
possible; staying for the optional find, the samples or the salvage is the gamble. This is the single biggest lever in the genre.

**Q3.** Rising pressure with time and noise? *(recommended)* — and should night bring the worst of it?

### 4. Nothing worth being greedy for
Credits come almost all from the contract. The optional find and samples are small. So there is little choice to make on site.

**Proposal:** **salvage worth carrying** in the buildings — instruments, cores, sealed cases, tools — each with a value and a weight,
some two-handed (no gun while carrying), sold at outposts. It turns every room into a decision and makes the two-handed rule matter.
(Phase 11B's wreck salvage would share it.)

**Q4.** Salvage in facilities now? *(recommended)* — or keep it for the wrecks in 11B?

### 5. Nothing needs two people
Everything can be done alone, so a crew splits up and does chores in parallel, and co-op is just faster solo.

**Proposal:** a few tasks that **want two at once**: hold a valve open while somebody else throws the pump; one reads the code off a
screen in one room while the other keys it in another (by voice); one keeps a light on a creature that hates it while the other works.
Always possible alone, slower or riskier.

**Q5.** Add two-person tasks? *(recommended: a few, never required)*

### 6. Every mission of a kind plays the same
Even with variants, a data mission is: find terminal, fix power, download, carry. After a few, nothing is new.

**Proposal:** more **ways a mission goes** within a kind (the data is on a body cam, in a lab's sample freezer, on a crashed drone
outside), plus **site events** now and then (a power surge trips everything, a door seals, a second crew's distress call, a
creature's nest found mid-mission). And contracts that ask for more than one thing at one place.

**Q6.** More variants and occasional events? *(recommended)* — any you would rule out?

### 7. Information is thin before you go down
The briefing gives the weather and whether there is a map. Choosing a contract is mostly by pay.

**Proposal:** the scan and the briefing carry **intel** that changes your plan: power state, what lives there (with sensors), a
building's condition, "a previous crew went in and did not report back". Kit choice then matters (the kit list helps).

**Q7.** Richer intel tied to sensors? *(recommended)*

### 8. Learning it
There is no tutorial yet (planned). Devices are learnt by trying.

**Q8.** Keep the tutorial for later as planned? *(recommended: yes, after the gameplay settles)*

## B. Smaller things noticed

- **Objectives text is long** (three lines of hint per step). Shorter steps, the detail on the device itself when up close.
- **The "OPTIONAL" records drive line** always shows, even far from it: show it only once its building is entered, as a find.
- **Debrief** could show time on site, noise made, what was left behind — the story of the run.
- **Dying alone far from the crew** is final for that run; a "downed, can be got up" state (idea 5 of the plan) keeps people together.
