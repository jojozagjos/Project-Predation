# Missions — what each is and how it works (2026-11-23)

Every site's mission comes from its kind (facility, station, survey ground, signal source; wrecks have none yet) and its seed, so
it is the same for everybody and on every visit. A place gone down to with no contract and no finding is only **Explore** (look
round, gather what is there, leave). Everything below is done **by hand**: devices worked up close with the pointer, things carried,
steps that go on by themselves once started (nobody has to stand next to anything — FIX 693), and noise that may bring something.

Common to every mission:
- **Briefing** at the ship's screens: destination, site, conditions, whether there is a map, and the objective — said and listed
  for how *this* mission is done (rack, archive, chain, finder…).
- **Map and objective tracker** (handheld devices). The tracker points at the next thing to do; it does not know a terminal with no
  map until somebody has found it, nor a live signal until it is tracked down.
- **Back to the shuttle** and launch from its console (a 20 s countdown; anyone not aboard is left).
- **Optional:** a records drive on a bench in another building (150 credits) -- or, about a third of the time, a part for the ship's
  upgrades instead (a drive coil, a sensor array, a servo pack: kept for the refit); survey markers brought back (30 each).
- **Kit** comes out of the ship's armoury at the loadout locker (Phase 8), so much of each as the ship has; restocked at outposts.
- **Tools** on the shuttle's tool shelf: a multitool (screws, boards) and a circuit tester (finds faulted breakers and fuses).

## 1. Recover the data (facility sites, about half)

Where the data is decides how it comes out:

**a) On a terminal (bench in a room).** Find it; press it to start the download (35–60 s; it carries on by itself, humming — heard
by whatever listens); take the drive off the bench and carry it back.
Complications that can stack on a terminal:
- **Power out** (4 in 10): the building is dark; its breaker board must be opened and the tripped breakers reset — and if one has a
  fault the main switch trips again until that one is found (circuit tester) and left off.
- **Feed cut** (4 in 10 of those): no supply at all — at the feeder cabinet outside by a door, isolate the main feed, turn the
  changeover to standby, switch the standby on.
- **Secured** (4 in 10): a keypad wants a four-figure code; it is written on a bright yellow note under a lit desk lamp on a bench
  in another room — an office, control room or lab where there is one. Once the terminal is found locked, the objective names that
  room and floor. (Never in a mine's cave.)
- **Supply faulted** (about 1 in 3): the terminal is dead though the building has power — the fuse box over its bench: four screws
  (multitool), isolator off, find the blown fuse (circuit tester), fit the spare from the cover, isolator on.

**b) In a server rack** (usually, where the building has a server room). Its drive bay is on a rack's face; up close, open its cover.
- **Pulled:** draw the drive out on its sled — it drops in front of the bay — carry it back.
- **Damaged:** the bay's lamp flashes red; draw the sled out to see the burnt board. Another rack in the building has a service hatch:
  open it, take its board out (multitool), bring it, fit it in the sled (lamp goes green), pull the drive.
- **Archive:** too big for a drive. The copy unit (14 kg) is on the bench in the shuttle's cabin: carry it to the rack, connect it to
  the yellow port, and it copies for 90 s by itself — loud, heard much further than a terminal. Disconnect it and carry it back.
  Needs the building's power (the breaker/feed steps above if it is out).

**c) Down a mine's cave** (where the site has one, more often than not): a survey station on a stand on the floor of its deepest dry
chamber, on the mine's power. No code, no fuse box.

## 2. Get the plant running (facility sites, the other half)

Restore the power if it is out (breaker, feed as above), then start the plant at its **control console** — three systems switched on in
order, each only once the one before is up to pressure (out of order, the lot trips).
**As a chain** (where the building has the rooms): before the console will start anything —
- **Fuel:** a fuel can (18 kg) on a bench in a room of its own → the generator's filler (a red tank on a wall over another bench):
  open its cap, pour (its lamp goes green).
- **Prime the pump:** its lever, eight strokes, the gauge needle rising (each stroke heard).
- **Valves:** four handwheels under a plate saying which are OPEN and which SHUT; found set otherwise.
Any order; each in a different room. A valve turned while the plant is coming up trips it.

## 3. Restore the station (station sites)

Its power is always out: the breaker board first. Then, where the station has the rooms (most do), its life support is put right by
hand before the console will restart it -- each in a room of its own, any order:
- **The CO2 scrubber:** a cabinet on a wall over a bench. Open its door, pull the spent cartridge out, and fit a fresh one, carried from
  a bench in another room (its lamp goes green).
- **The coolant pump:** its lever, eight strokes, the gauge rising.
- **The air valves:** four handwheels under a plate saying which are OPEN and which SHUT.
Then its life support restarted at the console (scrubbers, pumps, fans -- in order, each up to pressure before the next). A valve turned
while it comes up trips it. Back to the shuttle; nothing to carry.

## 4. Survey (survey ground)

The markers are in a crate on the shuttle cabin's right-hand bench; each survey point is an orange flag. Carry a marker out, plant it at
a flag, then up close: kick its three legs out, lengthen each leg's clamp until the bubble sits in its ring (the ground is uneven, new
each time), switch it on. Readings only come in while **three or more** are working at once (40 s each); working markers ping, which can
draw things. Something walking into a marker (a creature, a giant's foot) knocks it over: stand it up, legs out, level it again. Once all
are read, back to the shuttle — optionally switch each off, fold it, pull it up and carry it back aboard for 30 credits each.

## 5. Shut the signal down (signal sites, about half)

A transmitter somewhere on the site (out in the open, or indoors on a room's floor). The objective tracker does not know where it is:
the **Direction Finder** on the shuttle's bench does — its needle swings towards it, wide far off and settling close, its bars and beeps
stronger nearer. Once somebody has found it the tracker knows it. Up close: open its lid, switch the sender off, then the power (power
first goes off with a bang and changes nothing).

## 6. Bring the signal back (signal sites, the other half)

A transmitter gone silent — nothing for the finder to hear; the tracker has its last known position from the briefing. Up close: lid,
power on, then the sender.

## 7. Set up the beacon (survey ground, one in five)

Its parts -- mast, dish, power cell -- are in the crate in the shuttle, each taking both hands. Its place is the site's highest open
ground (a flag). Carry them up and fit them in order; then at the control box on the mast: a plate gives the aim, a readout shows where
the dish points, two knobs turn it round and up a step at a time, and the switch turns it on. On and aimed: back to the shuttle.

## 8. Bring back samples (survey ground, one in five)

Three outcrops, flagged. The cold case is on the bench in the shuttle: carrying it, drill a core at each (four turns, loud) straight
into it. Away from the shuttle it keeps them cold five minutes (counted down on the objective); aboard it chills again. Warmed through,
the cores spoil and all three are drilled again. Done with the case aboard holding all three.

## Not yet
- **Wrecks:** no missions until Phase 11B (crashed ship/station models).
- **A lost crew's last camp and its log** waits on you: its log is story, and it is to be found by following and working out, not
  pointed to.
