# Generation — what repeats, and what could be better (2026-11-23)

You asked for a list. Ranked roughly by how much each would change how places feel. Nothing here is built yet except where marked.

## Buildings

1. **Every building of a type looks the same outside.** Each facility type has one cladding palette and one window rhythm (labs dark
   with a red stripe, depots olive, and so on). Give each site its own variation: colour shift, window spacing, roof kit (vents, tanks,
   dishes), signage with the site's name, wear.
2. **Rooms are sized by the 3 m grid, not by what they are.** A washroom or a store can come out hall-sized with a few fittings along
   its walls; an office can be a corridor-shaped strip. Give each purpose a size range and proportion, split big rooms (stalls in a
   washroom, cubicles in an office, bays in a store), and merge or reuse leftovers (cupboards, a janitor's room, a plant closet).
   *Done now:* washrooms get a row of stalls (2–5), basins to match, a mirror and a second shower when big.
3. **Furniture only ever lines the walls.** Rooms read as empty in the middle. Layout templates per purpose: rows of desks in an
   office, island benches in a lab, bunks in pairs, tables in a mess, racks in rows in a server room (already), shelving aisles.
4. **No clutter.** Papers, mugs, tools, boxes, chairs pushed back, a jacket on a hook — small props by purpose and by how the place
   was left (abandoned in a hurry vs. cleared out). This is the biggest single fix for "it all feels the same".
5. **Corridors are all the same width and straight.** Some wider halls, a lobby or atrium, a dog-leg, a corridor through a
   workshop. Doors could be labelled with room names (functional text).
6. **Lighting is the same everywhere.** Warm in quarters, harsh in labs, dim and red in plant rooms, screens glowing in control rooms.
7. **Every door works.** Jammed doors (multitool), barricaded ones, collapsed corridors that force another way round — routing
   variety from the building's condition.

## Sites

8. **Outdoors is mostly scatter.** Each site type should have its own outdoor kit around and between buildings: a mine's headframe,
   conveyor and spoil heaps round the cave; a refinery's pipe racks between buildings; a comms relay's antenna field; a depot's
   container yard and gantry.
9. **Buildings sit apart in a ring.** Laying them along a road, round a yard, joined by covered walkways (some already) would make
   sites read as places built for a purpose.
10. **Few site types per kind.** More facility types (greenhouse/agri dome, water plant, prison/quarantine block, landing field with
    hangars) and mixed sites (a station next to a mine).

## Caves

11. **Mining sites: caves first.** *Done now:* a mine's cave is much bigger (a long way in, more branches, more caverns), more mine
    buildings break into one, and a mining site's mission goes down the cave far more often.
    Still to do: the workings themselves — rail tracks and ore carts, timber supports and lamps strung along the adits, worked faces
    with tools left, a cage lift down a shaft, an open pit mouth on the surface, flooded lower levels.
12. **Natural caves** feel like tubes: vary them with crawlways, chimneys, underground streams, crystal or ice chambers by biome.

# Second pass (2026-11-24): going through all of it, and what to ask you

The questions, each with what I recommend. The earlier list's 1-12 (above) are still open and folded in where they belong.

## What does not make sense

**G1. A sea-floor building's doors open on nothing.** Its doors are at its floor, metres over the sand on its legs (your picture). Under
water you swim to them, so it works, but it looks wrong. *Recommend:* only moon pools and lockouts as ways in on the sea's floor (no
plain doors), each lockout with a short ladder down its side to the sand.

**G2. Data and devices in rooms that would not have them.** A terminal is any bench in a room that "runs things", so data turns up in a
mess hall or a store. *Recommend:* data only in offices, control rooms, labs and server rooms; a plant's console only in a control or
plant room; a scrubber or pump in a plant or pump room where there is one.

**G3. Power is per building, with nothing behind it.** A building's power is out on its own breaker; the site has no power house, and
every other building is lit. *Recommend:* a site has a power house (generator or substation building); a building's power out is
sometimes its own breaker, sometimes the power house's -- another building to go to (ties to the cross-building wish).

**G4. Buildings sit in a ring round the pad.** Nothing joins them but worn paths (earlier list 9). *Recommend:* lay them along a road
or round a yard by site type, with the pad at the road's end, and the worn paths following it.

**G5. A site's kind and what is there do not always agree.** "Survey ground" with buildings standing on it; "station" (a ground
research station) easily confused with the space stations of Phase 11. *Recommend:* survey ground has at most a camp's huts; rename the
ground "station" kind to "research base" in what the player reads.

**G6. Ruined and burnt buildings are still chosen for work.** A plant is not started in a burnt building (already), but data is still
put in one half fallen in. *Recommend:* data in a ruin only when the contract says "recover from the damaged ...", never by chance.

## What would make places better (earlier list, to choose from)

**G7. Insides:** furniture laid out by room (desk rows, lab islands, bunks, mess tables) and clutter by how it was left (earlier 3-4).
The biggest single fix for "it all feels the same". *Recommend first.*

**G8. Outsides:** each facility type its own colours, roof kit and yard kit -- a mine's headframe and spoil heaps, a refinery's pipe
racks, a depot's container yard (earlier 1, 8). *Recommend second.*

**G9. Rooms sized by purpose**, big ones split (cubicles, store aisles), odd corners made cupboards (earlier 2).

**G10. Corridors and routes:** wider halls, an atrium, dog-legs, room signs on doors, jammed or collapsed ways forcing another way round
(earlier 5, 7).

**G11. Light by room:** warm quarters, harsh labs, dim red plant rooms, glowing control rooms (earlier 6).

**G12. Mines and caves:** rails and carts, timbering and strung lamps, a cage lift, flooded levels; natural caves with crawlways,
chimneys, streams, crystal or ice chambers by biome (earlier 11-12).

**G13. More kinds of place:** greenhouse domes, water plants, a quarantine block, a landing field with hangars; mixed sites (earlier 10).
