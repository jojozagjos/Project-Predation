# Generation — what repeats, and what could be better (2026-11-23)

You asked for a list. Ranked roughly by how much each would change how places feel. Nothing here is built yet except where marked.

## Buildings

1. **Every building of a type looks the same outside.** Each facility type has one cladding palette and one window rhythm (labs dark
   with a red stripe, depots olive, and so on). Give each site its own variation: colour shift, window spacing, roof kit (vents, tanks,
   dishes), signage with the site's name, wear.
2. **Rooms are sized by the 3 m grid, not by what they are.** A washroom or a store can come out hall-sized with a few fittings along
   its walls; an office can be a corridor-shaped strip. Give each purpose a size range and proportion, split big rooms (stalls in a
   washroom, cubicles in an office, bays in a store), and merge or reuse leftovers (cupboards, a janitor's room, a plant closet).
   *Done now:* washrooms get a row of stalls (2–5), basins to match, a mirror and a second shower when big.
3. **Furniture only ever lines the walls.** Rooms read as empty in the middle. Layout templates per purpose: rows of desks in an
   office, island benches in a lab, bunks in pairs, tables in a mess, racks in rows in a server room (already), shelving aisles.
4. **No clutter.** Papers, mugs, tools, boxes, chairs pushed back, a jacket on a hook — small props by purpose and by how the place
   was left (abandoned in a hurry vs. cleared out). This is the biggest single fix for "it all feels the same".
5. **Corridors are all the same width and straight.** Some wider halls, a lobby or atrium, a dog-leg, a corridor through a
   workshop. Doors could be labelled with room names (functional text).
6. **Lighting is the same everywhere.** Warm in quarters, harsh in labs, dim and red in plant rooms, screens glowing in control rooms.
7. **Every door works.** Jammed doors (multitool), barricaded ones, collapsed corridors that force another way round — routing
   variety from the building's condition.

## Sites

8. **Outdoors is mostly scatter.** Each site type should have its own outdoor kit around and between buildings: a mine's headframe,
   conveyor and spoil heaps round the cave; a refinery's pipe racks between buildings; a comms relay's antenna field; a depot's
   container yard and gantry.
9. **Buildings sit apart in a ring.** Laying them along a road, round a yard, joined by covered walkways (some already) would make
   sites read as places built for a purpose.
10. **Few site types per kind.** More facility types (greenhouse/agri dome, water plant, prison/quarantine block, landing field with
    hangars) and mixed sites (a station next to a mine).

## Caves

11. **Mining sites: caves first.** *Done now:* a mine's cave is much bigger (a long way in, more branches, more caverns), more mine
    buildings break into one, and a mining site's mission goes down the cave far more often.
    Still to do: the workings themselves — rail tracks and ore carts, timber supports and lamps strung along the adits, worked faces
    with tools left, a cage lift down a shaft, an open pit mouth on the surface, flooded lower levels.
12. **Natural caves** feel like tubes: vary them with crawlways, chimneys, underground streams, crystal or ice chambers by biome.
