# Project Predation
## Lore Reference

## Setting

Project Predation takes place in a grounded industrial science-fiction future.

Humanity has expanded beyond Earth and operates spacecraft, orbital stations, research facilities, industrial installations, and remote settlements.

The exact year is not important and should not be emphasized.

The world is technologically advanced, but it does not look sleek or magical. Much of the technology is practical, rugged, worn, and industrial.

A good visual comparison is:

> Advanced technology that still feels like an old car.

Ships and facilities may contain physical switches, warning lights, circuit breakers, rugged terminals, exposed conduits, service panels, patched equipment, and other machinery designed to be repaired and used for years.

The future still feels recognizably human.

People still have jobs, contracts, corporations, governments, maintenance problems, paperwork, arguments, and ordinary lives.

---

# CIRRA

## Critical Incident Response & Research Agency

Pronounced:

> **SEER-uh**

CIRRA is a massive private organization.

It is not technically a government agency, but it works closely with governments and receives government funding and contracts.

Because CIRRA controls or operates important infrastructure and performs critical services, it often has an authority that can feel almost governmental.

CIRRA operates both on Earth and throughout human-controlled space.

It may be involved in:

- emergency response
- scientific research
- infrastructure recovery
- hazardous incidents
- industrial disasters
- remote operations
- transportation
- facility recovery
- accident investigation
- biological incidents
- sensitive or classified research

CIRRA is broad enough that extraterrestrial life is only one part of what it deals with.

---

# “The Company”

CIRRA is commonly referred to simply as:

> **the Company**

Employees might say:

> “The Company wants the data.”

> “Company policy.”

> “The Company already knew.”

> “CIRRA wants this contained.”

The name should feel ordinary within the world.

CIRRA is large enough that people do not constantly explain what it is.

---

# CIRRA’s Image

CIRRA is not publicly presented as evil.

It appears professional, competent, useful, and necessary.

It can genuinely:

- save lives
- fund important research
- maintain infrastructure
- respond to disasters
- provide employment
- protect people

At the same time, CIRRA operates in a morally uncomfortable way.

It may:

- conceal information
- prioritize research over individual lives
- classify dangerous discoveries
- protect corporate interests
- treat some personnel as expendable
- manipulate information
- continue research that should arguably be stopped

Its darker side should feel institutional rather than theatrical.

CIRRA does not need to behave like a cartoon villain.

It simply has priorities, and individual people are not always at the top of that list.

---

# CIRRA’s Philosophy

One of CIRRA’s underlying beliefs is:

> **Knowledge creates control.**

If CIRRA encounters something unknown, its instinct is to understand it before destroying it.

It wants to:

- study it
- classify it
- recover it
- determine its value
- determine its danger
- understand how it works
- determine whether it can be controlled

This can be both reasonable and dangerous.

CIRRA may genuinely believe that research is necessary to protect humanity.

At the same time, the scientific and commercial value of the organisms may make CIRRA unwilling to simply destroy them.

---

# Extraterrestrial Life

Extraterrestrial life is known to exist.

Its existence is not necessarily treated as an unbelievable revelation.

People generally know that life beyond Earth exists or has been discovered.

However, most ordinary people do not regularly encounter dangerous extraterrestrial organisms.

The creatures at the center of Project Predation are unusual even within this world.

---

# The Organisms

The creatures encountered throughout Project Predation all share a common biological origin.

They are not simply unrelated alien species.

However, individual organisms can look and behave radically differently.

One specimen might be:

- humanoid
- quadrupedal
- multi-limbed
- elongated
- asymmetrical
- adapted to confined spaces
- adapted to climbing
- adapted to stalking
- physically small
- extremely large

Despite these differences, they are biologically connected.

This shared origin is not immediately obvious to ordinary people.

It becomes part of the deeper mystery surrounding the organisms.

---

# Adaptation

Adaptation is one of the organisms’ defining traits.

Their bodies and behavior can vary dramatically depending on circumstances.

Possible differences include:

- anatomy
- limb structure
- movement
- senses
- temperament
- hunting behavior
- defensive behavior
- environmental preference
- intelligence
- communication
- tolerance toward humans

The exact biological mechanism responsible for this adaptation has not yet been decided.

It is not yet established whether the differences come from mutation, metamorphosis, reproduction, environmental influence, or something else.

---

# The Creatures Are Not Evil

The organisms are living creatures, not supernatural monsters.

They can behave according to things like:

- fear
- hunger
- curiosity
- aggression
- territoriality
- self-defense
- predation
- learned behavior

Most can still be dangerous.

However, they are not automatically hostile simply because they are alien.

Some organisms might:

- observe humans
- retreat
- hide
- become curious
- defend themselves
- tolerate humans temporarily
- become aggressive only under certain circumstances

The uncertainty surrounding their behavior is part of what makes them frightening.

---

# Humans Are Not Automatically the Good Side

A major part of the tone is that humans are not automatically morally correct simply because the creatures are dangerous.

A creature might be frightened.

It might be defending itself.

It might have been captured.

It might be attempting to escape.

CIRRA may still classify it as dangerous and order it destroyed.

This creates ambiguity between:

> dangerous

and

> malicious.

Those are not always the same thing.

---

# The Tutorial Incident

One planned early story moment introduces this moral ambiguity.

The player encounters an alien organism during training or orientation.

The organism is capable of attacking, but it is also clearly frightened and attempts to get away.

It is effectively helpless compared with the player.

The player is ordered to kill it.

The scene helps establish CIRRA’s mentality.

To CIRRA, once something has been classified as a threat, specimen, liability, or unacceptable variable, its individual circumstances may no longer matter.

The moment is meant to feel uncomfortable rather than triumphant.

---

# What CIRRA Knows

CIRRA likely knows much more about the organisms than ordinary employees or the public.

CIRRA may already know or strongly suspect that very different-looking creatures share the same biological origin.

The player does not initially know everything CIRRA knows.

This creates a gap between:

> what CIRRA understands

and

> what CIRRA chooses to tell people.

The exact amount of knowledge CIRRA possesses is still undecided.

---

# The Shared-Origin Mystery

One of the larger mysteries of Project Predation is the realization that the different creatures are biologically related.

Clues could eventually come from things such as:

- shared tissue structures
- genetic similarities
- recurring biological patterns
- scientific reports
- old research
- conflicting classifications
- CIRRA records

The deeper realization is:

> These are not simply different alien species.

Something connects them.

---

# The Original Discovery

The exact origin of the organisms has not been decided.

It is currently unknown:

- where they were first discovered
- how humans found them
- how long CIRRA has known about them
- whether they were found dormant
- whether humans transported them
- whether they arrived independently
- whether CIRRA caused their spread
- when Project Predation began

This remains one of the biggest unanswered pieces of the lore.

---

# Why CIRRA Studies Them

CIRRA considers the organisms extremely valuable.

Their adaptive biology could have enormous scientific significance.

Possible areas of interest include:

- medicine
- tissue regeneration
- trauma recovery
- biotechnology
- new materials
- environmental adaptation
- industrial applications
- commercial advantage
- strategic or military interest

CIRRA may have several motivations at once.

It might genuinely believe:

> “We need to understand them to protect humanity.”

While also believing:

> “We cannot allow anyone else to control this discovery.”

---

# Project Predation

“Project Predation” can exist inside the world as CIRRA’s internal designation for its work involving these organisms.

The project may include:

- biological research
- specimen studies
- adaptation research
- incident records
- recovered biological material
- historical data
- classified research
- information about the organisms’ origin

The exact history of Project Predation has not yet been established.

---

# Facilities and Locations

Not every facility is a secret alien laboratory.

The world contains ordinary human infrastructure.

Locations may include:

- spacecraft
- space stations
- research installations
- industrial facilities
- arctic or ice facilities
- remote scientific sites
- corporate facilities
- other isolated infrastructure

The presence of an organism does not automatically mean CIRRA intentionally placed it there.

---

# Facility Incidents

Not every disaster is caused by a creature.

A facility might fail because of:

- mechanical failure
- human error
- negligence
- sabotage
- power failure
- structural damage
- environmental conditions
- research accidents
- evacuation
- poor decision-making
- an organism
- several problems happening at once

Sometimes the organism may be the cause.

Sometimes it may simply be another victim or consequence of the incident.

Sometimes humans may have created the dangerous situation themselves.

---

# Environmental Lore

The world’s story can be discovered through things such as:

- emails
- audio logs
- scientific notes
- maintenance records
- incident reports
- personal messages
- security records
- CIRRA communications
- damaged files
- handwritten notes
- employee notices

Not every piece of lore needs to be dramatic.

Ordinary details help make the world feel real.

A maintenance worker complaining about a broken pump can be as important to atmosphere as a scientist describing a dangerous specimen.

---

# Persistent Lore and Changing Facilities

Facilities may vary physically between visits while still having consistent underlying history.

The broad rule is:

> **The physical environment can change while the established story of that location remains consistent.**

For example, an arctic research station may always have the same core backstory.

Its exact room arrangement, accessible routes, and environmental layout may vary.

The events that happened there do not randomly contradict themselves.

---

# Lore Categories

The world’s lore can roughly be thought of in layers.

### World Lore
Information about human civilization, technology, governments, corporations, and CIRRA.

### CIRRA Lore
The company’s history, policies, research, secrets, and internal culture.

### Location Lore
The history of a specific station, facility, ship, or installation.

### Incident Lore
What happened during a particular disaster or failure.

### Creature Lore
Research and observations related to the organisms.

### Character Lore
Personal stories belonging to people who lived or worked at these locations.

---

# Dystopian Tone

The world is dystopian, but people living in it do not constantly call it dystopian.

The darkness comes from things being normalized.

Examples include:

- corporations controlling important infrastructure
- dangerous work being accepted as ordinary
- employees being treated as replaceable
- secrecy justified through contracts
- commercial interests influencing scientific decisions
- workers accepting questionable conditions because they need employment
- governments depending heavily on private corporations
- major organizations possessing enormous influence

The unsettling part is that people have learned to live with it.

---

# Human Life in the World

Even remote facilities should feel as though ordinary people actually lived and worked there.

Signs of normal life might include:

- personal photographs
- food
- notes
- shift schedules
- tools
- arguments between coworkers
- jokes
- employee complaints
- graffiti
- personal belongings
- reminders
- maintenance requests

The horror becomes stronger when it interrupts something recognizably human.

# CIRRA Personnel

CIRRA is not fundamentally a military organization.

It employs many kinds of people, including:

- researchers
- engineers
- technicians
- security personnel
- pilots
- medical personnel
- administrators
- emergency responders
- contractors
- executives
- scientists

The company is too large and too broad to be reduced to soldiers and scientists.

---

# Corporate Language

CIRRA sometimes uses calm institutional language to describe disturbing events.

For example:

Instead of:

> “A monster escaped.”

A report might say:

> “Specimen containment integrity was lost.”

Instead of:

> “Everyone died.”

It might say:

> “Personnel losses exceeded recovery thresholds.”

Instead of:

> “The creature attacked the station.”

It might say:

> “A hostile biological incident occurred.”

This kind of language helps reinforce CIRRA’s emotionally detached institutional culture.

---

# Central Thematic Conflict

One of the clearest thematic ideas in Project Predation is:

> **The organisms adapt. CIRRA categorizes.**

CIRRA tries to reduce the unknown into:

- classifications
- reports
- risk levels
- procedures
- research categories
- containment protocols

The organisms continually resist simple classification because they can vary so dramatically.

CIRRA believes that enough information will eventually make them predictable.

Whether that belief is correct remains uncertain.

# Current Lore in One Paragraph

**Project Predation** takes place in a worn, industrial science-fiction future where humanity has expanded beyond Earth without leaving ordinary human problems behind. A massive private organization called **CIRRA, the Critical Incident Response & Research Agency**, works closely with governments and industry and is commonly known simply as **the Company**. CIRRA handles major emergencies, research, infrastructure, and sensitive incidents across human-controlled space. Among its discoveries are a group of extraordinarily adaptable extraterrestrial organisms that can develop radically different bodies, behaviors, and temperaments while sharing a mysterious common biological origin. CIRRA knows more about these organisms than it publicly reveals and considers their biology too scientifically and economically valuable to ignore. The creatures themselves are not inherently evil, even though many are extremely dangerous. As more information is uncovered, the larger mystery becomes not only what these organisms are and where they came from, but how much CIRRA already knows and what humanity is willing to do in order to understand, control, and profit from them.
